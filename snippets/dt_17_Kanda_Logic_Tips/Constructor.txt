Constructor
Right now it’s still ~200 lines of one big __init__. It’s fully functional, but if you want to push SOLID even further you could split it into a few private methods:

class EEGVisualizerCore(QObject):
    def __init__(self, ...):
        super().__init__()
        self._init_signals()
        self._init_memory_monitor()
        self._init_folders()
        self._init_state()
        self._init_canvas()
        self._init_loader()
        self._init_amplitude_map_hooks()      
        self._init_ui_builders()
        # …

then move each block down into _init_* methods. This is purely stylistic but

    gives each chunk a name

    makes __init__ a clear orchestration of steps

    helps with future unit-testing (you can test _init_memory_monitor() in isolation, etc.)

Ampli­tude-Map setup
You still have two initialize_mcrvlt_snapshot_map methods in the class. You can collapse those two definitions into one, and/or move that whole sequence into a dedicated AmplitudeMapService class that the visualizer just calls.

Montage Switching
Your on_montage_changed still lives here. You might extract that into a small help_montage.py with:

def help01_on_montage_changed(viz, montage_value):
    # logic, side-effects

and let your class simply do:

def on_montage_changed(self, m):
    help01_on_montage_changed(self, m)

Splash / Focus
You could move show_kanda_splash and force_canvas_focus into their own SplashManager or FocusManager, but that’s more optional.

Remove leftover prints
Once you’re happy, you can sweep out most of the print("[DEBUG] …") calls and either replace them with proper logging.debug(...) or remove them altogether. That’ll slim the class further and let you lean on a central logging configuration.
